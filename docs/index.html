What it does

Plays almost any game

Cortex picks the right way to play for each game, so there's nothing to configure.

Free AI that understands any game

Add a free Google Gemini key (one minute, no card). Gemini looks at the game and plans the next few seconds: walk there, look around, open the menu, use that item.

Reacts in milliseconds

Keys and mouse never wait for the internet. Cortex asks Gemini for the next plan while it's still playing the current one, and fast moves you taught it run on your Mac in about 50 ms.

Teach it by playing

Press Record, play for a few minutes, then press F12. Cortex repeats what you showed it. It works fully offline, with no key at all, and Gemini can call your skills when they fit.

Just say what to do

Type it the way you'd tell a friend: follow the river and find a village, mine until the bag is full. The live view shows what Cortex sees and plans.

Any game, no setup

Open your game, click its window in Cortex, done. There's no game list to pick from and nothing to configure. 2D or 3D, it plays the window you choose.

You stay in control

F12 stops Cortex and F11 pauses it from inside the game. It also pauses when you switch to another window.

How it works

Two speeds, so it never waits

A cloud AI takes about a second to answer, which is too slow to steer every keypress. So Cortex splits the work.

~50 ms

Hands: on your Mac

Holds keys, turns the camera and clicks back-to-back. Skills you taught run here too: a small vision model (MobileCLIP) recognises the screen and does what you did, about 10 times a second.

~1 s

Brain: Gemini, in parallel

Every few seconds Cortex sends one screenshot to Gemini, which plans the next few seconds of moves. The request goes out before the current plan runs out, so the new plan is ready when it's needed.

The free Gemini tier allows about 15 requests a minute. Cortex stays under that by itself, and it keeps playing while it waits.

Install in four steps

1

Drag to Applications

Open Cortex.dmg and drag Cortex into the Applications folder.

2

Open it the first time

Open Cortex. If macOS blocks it, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

3

Follow the setup

Allow Screen Recording, Accessibility and Input Monitoring. Then paste your free Gemini key (optional).

4

Play

Open your game, click its window in Cortex, type what to do, press Start and switch to the game. F12 stops it.

Why the "Open Anyway" step? Apple only skips that warning for apps signed with a paid developer account. Cortex isn't signed yet. Its code is public on GitHub, and every download is built from it automatically.

Questions

Good to know

Which games does it work with?

Any single-player game that runs in a window on your Mac, 2D or 3D. Open it, click its window in Cortex, and say what to do. Games that need split-second precision, like fast shooters or rhythm games, work best with skills you teach it, because those react on your Mac.

Is it really free? How do I get the Gemini key?

Yes. Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey, sign in with a Google account, click Create API key, and paste it into Cortex's Settings. The free tier allows about 15 requests a minute and 1,000 a day, roughly an hour of play at the default pace. Lower the requests per minute in Settings to play longer.

What leaves my Mac?

With a Gemini key, Cortex sends your instruction and a small screenshot of the game window only to Google every few seconds while it plays. On the free tier, Google may use that data to improve its products. Without a key, nothing leaves your Mac. Keys are stored in the macOS Keychain.

Why does it need those permissions?

Screen Recording lets it see the game window. Accessibility lets it press keys and move the mouse. Input Monitoring makes the F12 stop and F11 pause keys work from inside the game.

Can I use it in online games?

Please don't. Bots break the rules of most online games and can get your account banned. Cortex is made for single-player and offline play.

Something went wrong. Where can I look?

Choose Settings → Show log in Cortex. Then open an issue with what happened and the end of the log.
